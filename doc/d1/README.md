Day 1: How a biological record is built
=======================================

**Wednesday 23 September 2026, 6 h of lectures.** No submission today. What you do with this
material is submitted tomorrow, as Q2 and Q3.

Yesterday's week dealt with where biological data come from and where they are deposited. Today we
open the deposits themselves. By the end of the day you should be able to take any biological record
you meet for the rest of the master's and answer four questions about it: what identifies it, what
it asserts, who asserted it, and what it can be joined to.

The two lectures are a pair. The morning stays inside a single record; the afternoon is about what
happens when records made by different communities, in different formats, under different curation
regimes have to be used together.

Lectures
--------

### [Lecture I: The anatomy of a biological record](lecture1.md)

**09:00-12:00.** Rows, columns and identifiers; sample sheets versus feature tables; the join as the
unit of analysis. The central dogma as a data pipeline, and the formats that carry each step: FASTA,
FASTQ, GenBank flat files, GFF. What an accession actually promises, what happens when a record
changes, and what submission looks like from the other side. One record, many serialisations, and
why interoperability is a format problem and an identifier problem at the same time.

### [Lecture II: From record to network](lecture2.md)

**14:00-17:00.** Occurrence data and Darwin Core; where occurrence records come from and what that
does to them; programmatic access to NCBI, UniProt, GBIF and BOLD. Why a name is not an identifier,
and what reconciliation services do about it. Ontologies as inference instruments, worked through
the Gene Ontology as a graph. FAIR in depth, with a live audit of a real deposit, plus provenance,
versioning, and the limits of what multi-source integration entitles you to claim.
